Model and policies

Model

Evidence conflicts: Winter 2012 found rounding SCr up (to 0.8 or 1.0) did not improve CG accuracy and actual SCr was better; Smythe 1994 found rounding to 1.0 underestimated CrCl in the elderly; Reichley 1995 found a 0.8 mg/dL minimum improved gentamicin K prediction; Dooley 2004 found ~0.68 helped only when CrCl ≤ 100. No floor is applied in spinal cord injury (the site's 0.5 rule was dropped).

Site model: min(IBW, TBW), no SCr floor, no obesity adjustment, no amputation correction, CrCl rounded to 0.01 mL/min, exactly as the site's calculator.

Body size

Cockcroft-Gault by weight basis

Highlighted row = the basis the active policy selects. CG was derived with actual weight in a largely non-obese cohort; IBW / AdjBW substitution is later practice.

Changing renal function (two SCr values)

Kinetic eGFR (Chen 2013) is a cross-check only, anchored to CG at the earlier SCr. Assumes the change began at or before the earlier SCr, and is not due to tissue breakdown or fluid shifts. If SCr is still rising, these underestimate the loss of function. SCr values are not floored here.

Pediatric (age < 18)

CKiD U25 (Pierce 2021; NIDDK-preferred) uses an age- and sex-dependent K with height in metres, ages 1–25. Bedside Schwartz 0.413 × height(cm)/SCr is for ages 1–16 with enzymatic SCr. Original Schwartz k values (0.45 infant, 0.55 child and adolescent female, 0.7 adolescent male) are valid only for Jaffe SCr. Values are per 1.73 m²; the dosing value is de-indexed (× BSA/1.73). Cockcroft-Gault is not valid under 18.

Methods and sources

IBW (Devine) = 50 kg (M) or 45.5 kg (F) + 2.3 kg per inch over 60 in, reduced by amputation %. AdjBW = IBW + 0.4·(TBW − IBW) (Schwartz 1978; Traynor 1995). FFM = Janmahasatian 9270·TBW/(6680 + 216·BMI) M, 9270·TBW/(8780 + 244·BMI) F. BSA DuBois.

Cockcroft-Gault: (140 − age)·Wt / (72·SCr), ×0.85 female. Literature weight rule (Winter 2012, Leader 1994): TBW if TBW < IBW; IBW if TBW ≤ 1.2·IBW; AdjBW if TBW > 1.2·IBW. At BMI ≥ 40 the reported value stays on AdjBW and CG with Janmahasatian LBW is shown as a comparison row (Demirovic 2009, Park 2012; Winter 2012 and Bouquegneau 2016 do not favour it). Spinal cord injury: no SCr floor; CG overestimates; optional CG × 0.8 (Chikkalingaiah 2010, PMID 20087164).

Chow-Schweizer (1985): solves S2 = S1·e^(−KT) + R/(K·Vd)·(1 − e^(−KT)), S in mg/L, Vd 0.6 L/kg, R = (28 − 0.2·age)·Wt/24 mg/h (×0.85 F); CrCl = K·Vd·1000/60. The site prints this equation without the 1/(K·Vd) term. Jelliffe / ASHP: as printed on the site, interval in days, result per 1.73 m² (and 0.86 female). Winter mass balance, dimensionally corrected. Chen kinetic eGFR (2013, PMID 23704286): SCr1·CG(SCr1)/mean SCr · (1 − 24·ΔSCr/(Δt·max ΔSCr/day)), max ΔSCr/day = production/Vd. Kinetic methods have low accuracy (Desgrouas 2021, Pelletier 2020); get drug levels.

Pediatric: CKiD U25 K (M) 39.0·1.008^(age−12) under 12, 39.0·1.045^(age−12) 12–17, 50.8 from 18; (F) 36.1·1.008^(age−12), 36.1·1.023^(age−12), 41.4; nearest integer age; reconstructed from Pierce 2021 text (PMID 33301749), supplement table not yet checked.

Normalized CrCl = CrCl × 1.73 / BSA. Drug models in this suite (except vancomycin K) take the un-normalized mL/min value.

Low muscle mass (cirrhosis, malnutrition, amputation, paralysis, frailty) makes SCr-based estimates overestimate function. Trimethoprim, cimetidine and similar drugs raise SCr without changing GFR. Prefer measured drug levels over predictions.

Labs and clinical findings

Points and boundary conventions

Boundaries are non-overlapping: a value exactly on a printed edge (bilirubin 2.0 and 3.0, albumin 3.5 and 2.8, INR 1.7 and 2.3, PT 4 and 6 s; cholestatic bilirubin 4 and 10) scores the middle band (2 points). µmol/L bilirubin is converted ÷ 17.1 and g/L albumin ÷ 10 before applying the mg/dL and g/dL cutoffs.

Suite drugs and the Child-Pugh class

    Methods and sources

    Child-Pugh (Pugh 1973, PMID 4541913): five criteria, 1–3 points each; total 5–15. Class A 5–6, B 7–9, C 10–15. The site's page uses fixed radio categories with overlapping labels and shows the class only as a legend; this page takes numeric labs and computes the class.

    INR cutoffs (< 1.7 / 1.7–2.3 / > 2.3) are a later convention, not in Pugh 1973, which used PT seconds over control (< 4 / 4–6 / > 6). Use one, not both. Cholestatic bilirubin cutoffs (< 4 / 4–10 / > 10 mg/dL) come from the PBC modification; applying them to PSC is a convention.

    Not validated for acute liver failure. FDA hepatic-impairment guidance (2003) uses Child-Pugh A/B/C for study design and labeling.

    Sources: specs/hepatic-score.md; lit/acyclovir-infusions-hepatic.md rows H1–H11 (argatroban label, Fresenius rev 3/2025; theophylline and phenytoin labels).

Estimates of renal and hepatic function are imprecise, especially with unstable renal function, extremes of body size or low muscle mass; confirm with measured levels and clinical judgement before dosing.
