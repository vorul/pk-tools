Methods, sources and education

Model. Linear one-compartment model; dosing weight = total body weight for both V and CL (site). k = CL/V, t½ = 0.693/k. LD = V·(Ctarget − Ccurrent)/(S·F). MD = Ctarget·τ·CL/(S·F). Css,avg = S·F·Dose/(CL·τ), Css,max = S·F·Dose/(V·(1 − e^(−kτ))), Css,min = Css,max·e^(−kτ).

Corrected site equations. Time to a fraction of steady state is −ln(1 − fss)/(k·24) days (the site omits the minus sign). From a known level: −ln[(1 − fss)/(1 − Ccur/Css)]/(k·24). Non-steady-state level after N doses, T h after the last: S·F·Dose·(1 − e^(−kNτ))·e^(−kT)/(V·(1 − e^(−kτ))) (the site prints 1*Exp). With an irregular history the page sums each dose separately (superposition).

Two parameter sets. Literature (default, signed off 2026-10-23): neonate (< 2 wk) CL 0.0047 L/kg/h, V 0.96 L/kg (Grasela & Donn 1985); infant 2 wk–< 1 y CL 0.006 (low confidence; Yukawa 2005, Marsot 2014); child 1–< 12 y 0.008 (Winter; Botha 1995 0.0076); adolescent 12–< 15 y 0.006 and 15–< 19 y 0.005 (low confidence); adult 0.004 (Winter; Messina 2005 controls 0.0041); > 65 y 0.0032 (Messina 2005); V 0.7 L/kg after the neonatal period. Reference range 10–40 mg/L (Patsalos 2018), dose-solve target 20 mg/L, Winter 15–40 shown as the usual therapeutic overlay. Site (reproduces the site workbook, phenobarbital.xlsm): V 0.96 / 0.63 / 0.61 L/kg; CL = dosing-table MD ÷ (20 mg/L × 24 h): 0.00625 (< 2 wk), 0.01042 (2 wk–< 1 y), 0.00938 (1–< 5 y), 0.0075 (5–< 10 y), 0.00604 (10–< 15 y), 0.00521 (15–< 19 y), 0.00417 (19–65 y), 0.00323 L/kg/h (> 65 y); valproate ×0.75 at 1000 and ×0.5 at 2000 mg/day; range 10–40 mg/L. The page's primary list (0.0047 / 0.0082 / 0.0056 / 0.0024) is not used by the workbook. Ages: ≥ 19 y is adult; > 65 y is older adult.

Salt and bioavailability. Sodium phenobarbital injection S = 0.91 (232.24/254.22 = 0.9135); tablets and elixir S = 1. F = 1 for IV, IM and oral; 0.9 rectal: the injectable sodium solution given rectally, so S·F = 0.91 × 0.9 = 0.82 (Graves 1989, relative F 90% vs IM). A commercial suppository would use F 1 and its own salt factor.

Interactions. Valproate lowers phenobarbital clearance 25% per 1 g/day, floor ×0.5: max(0.5, 1 − 0.25·g/day) (Vučićević 2015; Winter Q7; mechanism Kapetanović 1981). Site mode: ×0.7 at 1000 and ×0.5 at 2000 mg/day, linear between. Chloramphenicol ×0.6; pyridoxine ×1.4; thioridazine/mesoridazine/phenothiazines ×1.3; carbamazepine ×0.91 and phenytoin ×0.8 in patients over 65 (site). Phenobarbital induces CYP1A2, 2A6, 2B6, 2C8, 2C9, 3A4, 2D6 and P-gp: review every co-medication.

Non-steady-state fit. Each dose is a bolus summed by superposition. MAP objective Σ((obs − pred)/(0.1·obs))² + Σ((θ − θpop)/(CV·θpop))², Levenberg-Marquardt with Nelder-Mead fallback (pkcore PK.fit). Least squares drops the prior; with one level only CL is fitted. The Site model defaults to the workbook's Bayesian fit: V fixed, normal CL prior with CV 0.1, residual 10% of the level, K kept within 0.1–10 × population. A level drawn at or before the first dose is used as the starting level and decays from its draw time. When a level is above target, days to hold = ln(C/Ctarget)/(24·k).

Sampling. Peak 2–3 h after a load (after distribution) to decide on further loading. Routine monitoring uses troughs. Half-life is long (adults about 4–5 days, children about 2–3 days), so steady state takes 2–4 weeks: check weekly levels during initiation.

Safety. Concentration-related effects: sedation, impaired cognition, ataxia; in neonates poor feeding, reduced respirations and tone; coma at high levels (> 100 mg/L may be lethal). Loading doses can cause respiratory depression and hypotension, especially after benzodiazepines: monitor respiration and oxygen saturation. Long-term: folate and vitamin D deficiency, neonatal vitamin K deficiency, connective-tissue effects. Refractory status epilepticus (≥ 70 mg/L) and barbiturate coma (> 75 mg/L) need ICU protocols and are not computed here.
