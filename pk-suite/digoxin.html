Advanced: equations and policies

Literature model (approved sign-off): Reuning Vd 3.8·Wt + 3.1·CrCl and Sheiner CL (Winter, Basic Clinical Pharmacokinetics ch. 3), Wt = min(IBW, TBW), absolute Cockcroft-Gault CrCl (Chow when SCr is changing), no intermediate rounding, oral first-order absorption ka 1.6 h⁻¹ (no lag), IV 0.25 h input, MAP priors log-normal CV 35 % with residual 0.15 ng/mL + 10 %. Paulson-Welling, (4.5 + 0.028·CrCl per 1.73 m², BSA from ABW)·Wt, is a selectable alternative src. Site model: reproduces DigoxinDosing.asp (Reuning Vd with 3.12, Sheiner CL, LBW = min(IBW, ABW) rounded to 0.1 kg, CrCl, Vd and CL rounded to 0.1, K to 4 dp, LD/MD to 0.001 mg, target 1 ng/mL, lb ÷ 2.2). The site's own Excel tools use Paulson Vd and give 2–30 % smaller Vd and loading doses than its web calculator.

Workbook model: reproduces the site's Excel tools (digoxinweb.xlsm and the K/VD fitting workbook; spec §13). Paulson-Welling Vd; Sheiner CL with the interaction factor applied once (digoxinweb applies it twice in CHF; that bug is not reproduced); one interaction (the strongest); LBW = min(IBW, ABW), Peck under 18 y or 48 in; SCr floor 0.7 mg/dL in adults (0.5 with paralysis/malnutrition); Chow for changing SCr; no rounding; every dose is a zero-order input of the time set above (default 0.25 h); no Φ; capsule F 1. Fitting: normal priors on K and V (CV 30 % each), residual 10 % of the observed level, bounds K 0.1–10 × and V 0.5–2 × population; least squares is unweighted. How it differs from the web calculator (Site model): Paulson instead of Reuning 3.12 Vd, an SCr floor, 0.25 h instead of 1 h oral input, no rounding chain, no Φ, and a K-and-V Bayesian fit instead of a K-only least-squares fit. The prior and residual fields above apply to the literature and site models only. Not ported: the per-interval K intercept 0.014 h⁻¹, the days/hours mix-up in "assume steady state", and the patient name/MRN export.

About digoxin dosing (educational)

Digoxin follows linear one-compartment kinetics once distribution is complete (4–6 h after IV, 6–8 h or more after oral). Levels drawn earlier are falsely high and cannot be fitted. Draw at least 4–6 h after IV and 6–12 h after oral doses; just before the next dose is best. Earlier levels are excluded from fitting.

Half-life is about 1.5–2 days with normal renal function and 3.5–6 days in anuria, so steady state takes 1–3 weeks. A level drawn earlier is not at steady state; use the dose-history (superposition) fit.

Targets: heart failure 0.5–0.9 ng/mL (aim 0.7; DIG trial post hoc, Rathore 2003, Ahmed 2006; ESC 2021 HF: aim <1.2); AF rate control <1.2 ng/mL (2023 ACC/AHA/ACCP/HRS, class 2a; ARISTOTLE). The site's older ranges (calculator trough 0.8–1.2 HF and 1.5–2 AF; info page 0.5–0.8 HF and 0.5–2 AF) are kept as selectable options only.

Loading doses are usually unnecessary in chronic heart failure. For AF they are given as 50%, 25%, 25% six hours apart (label 10–15 mcg/kg lean weight orally, 8–12 mcg/kg IV).

Toxicity is more likely with hypokalaemia, hypomagnesaemia, hypercalcaemia, hypothyroidism and falling renal function, and can occur in the "normal" range. Signs: nausea, vomiting, visual colour changes, confusion, bradycardia, AV block and ventricular arrhythmias.

Interactions are modelled as CL multipliers derived from the Lanoxin label (amiodarone 0.5, quinidine 0.5 with Vd 0.7, verapamil 0.6, dronedarone 0.4, propafenone 0.6, itraconazole 0.55, clarithromycin 0.6, ranolazine 0.67, carvedilol 0.75, canagliflozin 0.8). The site uses verapamil 0.75, dronedarone 0.5, propafenone 0.75 and azoles 0.5. Thyroid ±30 % on CL and V is approximate. Heart failure status "Unknown" uses the CHF equation (conservative, Winter); no extra flat reduction is applied.

Site-parity note: the site's displayed peak (CmaxssExact) is 0.1–0.7 % below the exact steady-state superposition by an unexplained factor Φ(K). The site model applies the empirical fit −ln Φ ≈ 0.134·K + 13·K²; turn it off in Advanced.

For educational use and clinical decision support by trained clinicians only. Results are model estimates from population parameters and limited levels: check timing, adherence, renal function, electrolytes, interactions and the clinical picture, and confirm every dose independently. Not a substitute for professional judgement.
