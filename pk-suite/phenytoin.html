Methods and sources

Units. Every field has its own unit; click the unit to switch it, and the value converts. CA, US and UK set every field and the results to that country's usual units. The model runs in mg/L, g/dL, inches and kg. Phenytoin µmol/L = mg/L × 3.964 (MW 252.27): 10–20 mg/L is 40–80 µmol/L, free 1–2 mg/L is 4–8 µmol/L. Valproic acid µmol/L = mg/L × 6.934. Albumin g/L ÷ 10 = g/dL. Km stays in mg/L.

Salt factor. S = 0.92 for phenytoin sodium capsules, IV phenytoin and fosphenytoin (mg PE); S = 1.0 for chewable tablets and suspension. F = 1. Products are not interchangeable mg for mg.

Model setting. Literature defaults (the default) use the values recommended in the suite literature review: Anderson 0.25 binding (0.29 in ICU, Soriano 0.2 in ESRD) and Vmax 6.0 mg/kg/day at 60 y and older. Site workbook reproduces pkineticdrugdosing.com and its workbooks: Winter binding and Vmax 7 at 60 y and older. The active model is shown on every result card. Values marked verify come from outside the site, from secondary sources, or are suite policy; hover a badge for its source.

Binding, with factor = measured total ÷ normal-binding level and CNB = C / factor (Alb in g/dL). Literature default: Anderson 1997 0.25·Alb + 0.1; ICU (Kane 2013) 0.29·Alb + 0.1; dialysis or CrCl < 10 mL/min (Soriano 2017) 0.2·Alb + 0.1, always with a mandatory free-level banner. CrCl 10–25 without dialysis keeps the standard equation, because binding there is unpredictable rather than ESRD-like (Winter, Liponi 1984); a free level is recommended, and required with albumin below 3.5 g/dL. The legacy ESRD forms (Winter 0.48, 0.1·Alb + 0.1) are options only: they over-predict the free fraction by 75–200% in dialysis patients (Soriano 2017; Palumbo 2026, PMID 42557917). These coefficients were confirmed through a secondary source (ClinCalc), so they carry a verify badge. Selectable: Winter (Eq 14.2, 14.3) 0.9·Alb/4.4 + 0.1, hemodialysis 0.9 × 0.48 × Alb/4.4 + 0.1; classic Sheiner-Tozer 0.2·Alb + 0.1 (ESRD 0.1·Alb + 0.1). The factor is capped at 1, so normal albumin is never corrected upward (suite policy). Measured free level: factor = 0.1 ÷ (free/total). Valproate > 20 mg/L (Eq 14.8): (0.095 + 0.001 × VPA) × C / 0.1. No equation is reliable in ICU patients or in renal failure with low albumin (Montgomery 2019, Barra 2020), so the tool asks for a measured free level there. Patient-specific reported range is 10–20 mg/L × the binding factor (Eq 14.4–14.7). Every fit uses binding-corrected levels.

Weights and volume. IBW (Devine) 50 (M) or 45.5 (F) + 2.3 kg per inch over 60 (actual weight under 152 cm). Vd = 0.65 L/kg (1 L/kg under 1 year); above IBW, IBW + 1.33(TBW − IBW). Vmax per kg uses the lesser of IBW and actual weight.

Population values (Murphy 2005, from your workbook): 0.5–3 y Km 6.6 (SD 4.2), Vmax 14 (4.2); 4–6 y 6.8 (3.5), 10.9 (3); 7–9 y 6.5 (3), 10.1 (2.6); 10–17 y 5.7 (2.7), 8.3 (2.8); 18–59 y 4.3 (3.5), 7 (3); ≥ 60 y 5.8 (2.3), 7 (3) in the site model; the literature model uses Vmax 6.0 (SD 1.9) at 60 y and older (Bauer & Blouin 1982, studied at 60–79 y). There are no data at 80 y and older: 6.0 is kept, flagged, with a prior CV of at least 0.35. The pediatric values match Bauer & Blouin 1983. Winter assumes Km 4 for adults. CYP2C9 intermediate and poor metabolizers: Vmax × 0.75 and × 0.5.

One level (Eq 14.30–14.31): Vmax = R(Km + C)/C, R = S·F·Dose·24/τ; dose Vmax·T/((Km + T)·S); Css = Km·R/(Vmax − R). Two steady-state levels (Eq 14.36): Km = (R2 − R1)/(R1/C1 − R2/C2). Not at steady state: mass balance (Eq 14.33–14.34) eliminated/day = R − (C2 − C1)·V/t, Vmax = eliminated × (Km + Cavg)/Cavg; or a Bayesian fit minimizing ((C2 − C2pred)/(0.1·C2))² + ((Km − Kmpop)/SD)² + ((Vmax − Vmaxpop)/SD)². After stopping (Eq 14.25): Vmax = V·[Km·ln(C1/C2) + (C1 − C2)]/t. Two steady-state pairs whose level does not rise more than proportionally to the dose are rejected (the workbook hides them with ABS()).

Other one-level methods. Bayesian, population priors: MAP fit of Km and Vmax to one steady-state level, ((C − Css)/(0.1·C))² + ((Km − Kmpop)/SD)² + ((Vmax − Vmaxpop)/SD)². This is the computed version of Winter's orbit graph. In the literature model this is the default single-level method, with fixed Km 4 (Winter) shown as a cross-check. Graves-Cloyd (Graves NM, Leppik IE et al., Ther Drug Monit 1986, PMID 3824428): Dnew = Dold·(Cnew/Cold)0.2. It under-predicts on average, so the dose is rounded up. Bauer's form with exponents 0.199/0.804 is the same equation. Timing estimates use the Km and Vmax of the curve through the current point and the Graves point.

Time-based Bayesian (site workbook "Bayesian 2NonSteadyStateLevels"): fits the time between two levels, ((Tobs − Tpred)/(0.1·Tobs))² + ((Vmax − Vmaxpop)/(0.4·Vmaxpop))² + ((Km − Kmpop)/(0.4·Kmpop))², with Tpred the closed-form time between levels. The site prior is Vmax 7 mg/kg/day; the literature model uses the age-group Vmax. Fits are solved with the suite optimizer (Levenberg-Marquardt), which finds the true minimum the workbook's saved Solver result misses.

Dose history (explicit workbook; Goličnik 2011, Tang & Xiao 2007). Each dose is an instant bolus. Between doses C(t) = Km·W((C0/Km)·e(C0 − (Vmax/V)·t)/Km), with W the Lambert W function solved exactly (the workbook uses an approximation good to 0.2%). Bolus steady state: the peak P solves C(P, τ) + S·D/V = P (equal to the workbook's Z), and trough = P − S·D/V. The MAP fit uses the workbook priors (CV 0.4 on Km, Vmax/V and, optionally, Vd) and a level error of 0.1 × observed. Fitted values outside 0.2–1.8 × population (the workbook Solver bounds) are flagged. Fit Vd only when a peak is available.

Loading (Eq 14.28, 14.32, 14.29): LD = V·(Cdesired − Cobserved)/S; after IV C0 = S·LD/V; oral peak about 0.5·S·LD/V. Binding does not change the loading dose. Maximum IV rates: phenytoin 50 mg/min (25 elderly or cardiac; children 1 mg/kg/min), fosphenytoin 150 mg PE/min (children 3 mg PE/kg/min). Oral loads: ≤ 400 mg per dose every 2 h. Sample 2 h after IV phenytoin, 4 h after fosphenytoin, 24 h after an oral load. Switch (Eq 14.35): new dose = dose × Sold/Snew. CRRT (Eq 14.38): removed mg/day = unbound level × effluent L/h × 24.

Timing and rounding. Steady-state check (Eq 14.24) (115 + 35C)·C/(R × 70/wt) days; t90 (Eq 14.23); time between levels integrates V·dC/dt = R − Vmax·C/(Km + C). Recheck day 3 (dose ≥ 85% of Vmax, > 15% increase, or predicted > 18 mg/L) or day 5, then at the model's 90% time (≥ day 7). Doses round to 100 + 30 mg capsule combinations (≤ two 30 mg per dose, doses within 30 mg), whole 50 mg chewables, or 25 mg steps, choosing the predicted level closest to target within 10–20 mg/L.

Not modeled. Interacting drugs, enteral feeds, hepatic disease, pregnancy, neonates, oral absorption (the dose-history model treats doses as instant). Shared code. Body weights, Cockcroft-Gault, rounding and the fitting engine come from the PK Suite library (lib/pkcore.js). The creatinine field only checks the renal-status choice; doses don't depend on it.

Educational use. This is a teaching and decision-support aid for trained clinicians, not a substitute for clinical judgement, local protocols or a pharmacist review. Outputs are model estimates from limited levels and population assumptions, and some defaults still need clinical sign-off (marked verify). Check adherence, timing, binding and interactions, and recheck levels after any change.
