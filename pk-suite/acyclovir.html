Methods and sources

Model setting. Literature (default) applies the clinician-approved sign-off decisions (DECISIONS.md, decisions/acyclovir-infusions.md, 2026-10-23) to the current FDA label (DailyMed setid ae921e4e, 7/2023) and IDSA 2008. Dosing weight is AdjBW (IBW + 0.4 × excess) when TBW > 1.2 × IBW or BMI ≥ 30, otherwise TBW, with a one-click IBW (label) option. Renal bands are compared per 1.73 m² (label units), with absolute mL/min shown beside them. IHD is 2.5–5 mg/kg q24h after HD. CRRT is 10 mg/kg q12h for CNS, VZV or severe infection, otherwise 5–7.5 mg/kg q24h. Other choices: 1 h minimum infusion, smallest bag at or below 7 mg/mL, HSV encephalitis 14–21 days, VZV encephalitis 10 mg/kg (15 mg/kg option) for 10–14 days. Site reproduces pkineticdrugdosing.com: AdjBW (f 0.4) for everyone above IBW, 2 h infusion, 250 mL for 10 mg/kg doses or doses over 500 mg, HSV encephalitis 10 days (label duration). Both use the label renal table, the label 20 mg/kg q8h maximum and nearest-25 mg rounding.

Weights. Devine IBW 50 (M) or 45.5 (F) + 2.3 kg/in over 60 in; AdjBW IBW + f·(TBW − IBW), f 0.4 (site) or 0.3; BMI; DuBois BSA. All from PK.body. Where a policy picks IBW and TBW is lower, TBW is used. The label says obese patients are dosed on IBW. Evidence on IBW vs AdjBW is mixed (Turner 2016 PK favours AdjBW; Saad 2025 shows the least AKI with IBW; Zelnicek 2023 the reverse). TBW is never appropriate in obesity (TBW > 1.2 × IBW or BMI ≥ 30).

Breakpoint discontinuity. With an "AdjBW above a BMI breakpoint, TBW below" rule, a patient must weigh ΔW = (Wbp − IBW)·(1/f − 1) more than the breakpoint weight before the dose matches (32.8 kg at BMI 30 for a 70 in male).

Renal adjustment (label Table 5, CrCl mL/min/1.73 m²): > 50 100% q8h; 25–50 100% q12h; 10–<25 100% q24h; < 10 50% q24h. Hemodialysis: 50% q24h, with the dose given after each dialysis (label: HD t½ about 5 h, a 6 h run lowers levels about 60%, an additional dose after each dialysis). Peritoneal dialysis: 50% q24h, no supplement. Those are the Site-model rules. The Literature model uses the approved rules instead. IHD: 2.5–5 mg/kg q24h (half the indication mg/kg), given after HD on dialysis days (Heintz 2009; Krasny 1982). CRRT (not in label; Heintz 2009, Trotman 2005, Li 2020; assumes effluent 1–2 L/h): 10 mg/kg q12h for CNS, VZV or severe infection, otherwise 5–7.5 mg/kg q24h. In Site mode CRRT shows the indication mg/kg (capped at 10) q12h. When the per-1.73 m² and absolute CrCl fall in different bands, a warning is shown. CrCl is Cockcroft-Gault from PK.renal.cockcroftGault (literature preset: AdjBW if TBW > 1.2 × IBW, otherwise the lesser of IBW and TBW; site preset: lesser of IBW and TBW; no SCr floor), or entered directly.

Administration. Label: constant-rate infusion over at least 1 h, never bolus; final concentration about 7 mg/mL or lower; adequate hydration. The site prefers 2 h and 250 mL bags; concentrations above 5 mg/mL get a soft warning (suite preference). Urine output target 100–150 mL/h (about 1.5 mL/kg/h) and IV fluids over 2 L/day are site and Kim 2015 guidance.

Exposure (estimate only). Blum 1982: CL (L/h/1.73 m²) = 0.2022·CrCl1.73 + 1.722, scaled by BSA/1.73. The coefficients could not be checked against the full text; the intercept matches the label's anuric CL (29 mL/min/1.73 m²). Volume: Literature default 0.6 L/kg × dosing weight (site page text); Site model 48 L/1.73 m² × BSA/1.73 (Blum Vss, as the site workbook and spec §5 use); either can be chosen under Dosing policy. One-compartment steady-state peak, trough and AUC24 from PK.one.ss. On HD or PD the interdialytic CL is the anuric intercept. Spector (0.091·CrCl + 7.52) and Fletcher (0.178·CrCl + 2.92) are Pierce's own LINEST fits to the tabulated patients in those continuous-infusion papers (Spector with CG-recomputed CrCl), not published equations, and are shown for comparison only. Predicted urine concentration (site workbook method): hourly amount fe·V·(C(t) − C(t+1)) ÷ urine flow, mean during the infusion fe·(dose − V·(Cmax − Cmin))/T' ÷ flow, and continuous fe·rate ÷ flow; defaults fe 0.9 and 100 mL/h; flagged above the 1.3 mg/mL solubility (Hintz 1982). Renal band at exactly CrCl 10: the label's ranges overlap; the Literature model gives 100% q24h, the Site model 50% q24h as the workbook does. MW 225.2: 1 µmol/L = 0.225 mg/L.

Durations. HSV mucocutaneous (immunocompromised) 7 d; initial genital herpes 5 d; VZV immunocompromised 7 d (label). HSV encephalitis 14–21 d (IDSA, Tunkel 2008; label 10 d). VZV encephalitis 10–15 mg/kg q8h for 10–14 d (IDSA, not verified this cycle).

Warnings. Crystal nephropathy in 10–48% (onset 24–72 h); risk rises with dehydration, rapid infusion, concentrated bags, CKD, diabetes, obesity (OR 3.2), vancomycin (OR 4.7), NSAIDs (OR 2.6) and higher daily doses (Barber 2019). Neurotoxicity in about 1% of IV patients (label): confusion, tremor, hallucinations, seizures; more likely with renal impairment, unadjusted doses, obesity dosed on TBW, older age and underlying neurologic disease. TTP/HUS has been fatal in immunocompromised patients (label).
